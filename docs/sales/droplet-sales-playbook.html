Sell the server closet to the independent dental practice

Built from a read of the three repos: droplet-onboard-services (the box), DropletAgent (Mac client) and droplet-windows (Windows client and VPN service). Every claim below is backed by a file in those repos, cited in grey.

The customer group
Independent, owner-led dental practices with 2–6 dentists and 10–40 staff, running Open Dental, Dentrix or Eaglesoft on premises, with no in-house IT and an outsourced dental-IT provider.

The owner dentist signs. The office manager uses it every day. The practice's dental-IT provider decides whether it gets installed.

Ideal customer profile

Who to call, and who to skip

Firmographics

  • 1–3 locations, 10–40 staff. The setup wizard defaults to 11–50. OrgStep.tsx
  • Owner dentist is still chairside and still signs checks
  • Not owned by a DSO. DSOs buy centrally and want SOC 2 first

Technology

  • Practice system on a server in the building, not cloud-only
  • Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, so SSO works on day one ONBOARDING_SSO_OIDC.md
  • QuickBooks for the books quickbooks connector
  • Old cameras, a consumer router and a NAS that "the IT guy" set up

Trigger events

  • The server, NAS or recorder is due for replacement
  • Staff caught pasting patient notes into ChatGPT
  • A ransomware scare at a practice nearby, or a cyber-insurance questionnaire
  • Opening a second location

Skip them if

  • The practice system is cloud-only (Dentrix Ascend only, Curve). Most of the box's value goes away
  • The buyer asks for a SOC 2 report up front. None exists yet compliance-progress.md
  • There is no local IT provider willing to rack the box

Positioning

What we say

"An AI office manager that knows your practice, in a box that never lets patient data leave the building. It also replaces your server, your router and your camera recorder."
  • Lead with the morning huddle, not with security. Security is why they can say yes. The daily brief is why they want it.
  • Say "stays in the building", never "HIPAA compliant". HIPAA readiness, the BAA template and SOC 2 are not started. The repo's own Trust page says nothing on it is a claim yet. apps/web-dashboard/src/app/trust/page.tsx
  • Show the approval card. "Reads run automatically, writes ask for a thumbs-up, destructive actions are blocked." This answers "what if the AI does something wrong?" before anyone asks. DropletAgent/CLAUDE.md hard rule 2
  • Sell consolidation. The practice already pays for a server, a firewall, a recorder and backups. Price the lease against that combined line, not against a ChatGPT seat.
Channel

Sell through the dental-IT provider

ADR-042 describes the customer as "a business defined by not having an IT department". Those businesses rent one. Almost every independent practice has a regional dental-IT shop that owns the closet, and that shop can block any hardware sale. The repos have no reseller or MSP channel today.

  1. Recruit 3–5 regional dental-IT providers as installation partners. They rack the box, run the setup wizard and keep the support relationship. Pay them a share of the lease for the full two-year term. 2-year lease cycle: CLAUDE.md Foundation
  2. Use study clubs and county dental societies for demand. Run one 30-minute live demo of the morning brief, on a real box, at a monthly meeting.
  3. Stay independent of Patterson and Henry Schein. They own Eaglesoft and Dentrix and sell competing products. Patterson has not sanctioned direct database access. docs/integrations/eaglesoft.md
The motion

From first call to signed lease

  1. Discovery call, 20 minutes. Ask four questions. When does your server or recorder need replacing? Who is your IT provider? Has anyone on staff used ChatGPT with patient information? How do you prepare the morning huddle today?
  2. Site visit. Stand up export-drop on the first visit. It is the only practice-system integration that needs no vendor approval, because the practice exports its own reports to a folder it controls. export-drop.md §1
  3. 30-day paid pilot. The office manager gets a morning brief from the practice's own data every day. Measure one number: unscheduled treatment put back on the books.
  4. Convert to the two-year lease. Credit the pilot fee toward the lease. The dental-IT partner does the full install: network, cameras and file migration.
  5. Reference. Ask for one study-club talk by the owner. One dentist presenting to other dentists is the sales tool that matters in this market.

The first 3 practices are design partners, not just customers. Every export-drop profile is marked unverified until someone checks it against a real export (Dentrix ships no profile). Those practices provide the real exports. Discount them in return. export-drop.md "Built-in profiles"

Objections

What they will say

"Will you sign a BAA?"Not until the template exists; that work is not started. Until then, answer with the architecture: the box is leased hardware in their building, and there are no Warp-operated servers holding their data. Have counsel write the BAA before the first dental meeting. compliance-progress.md · ADR-028
"We already have Microsoft 365. Why not Copilot?"Copilot cannot see the practice system on the server in the back room. Droplet reads it on the LAN and still connects to their M365. apps/orchestrator/src/services/m365/
"My IT guy will say no."Put the IT guy in the deal. He installs it, earns a share of the lease and keeps the client.
"Is the local AI good enough?"Demo on their own data. Today's box runs a 20B model on a 16 GB card with a 16k context. Keep demos to briefs, lookups and drafts, and avoid long multi-document reasoning. docs/dmr-single-box.md · config.ts
"What happens when the lease ends?"A new box on the 2-year cycle, with signed updates in between. The data stays theirs, encrypted at rest with a TPM-sealed key. docs/security/at-rest-encryption.md
Gates

Fix these before the first signature

These are what the repos show is missing. Each one can lose a deal.

Blocker

Set a price. The docs disagree: CLAUDE.md says a 2-year lease, while ADR-026 says a "one-time-purchase appliance". ROADMAP still lists "No defined revenue model" as a risk. Pick the lease, set a monthly figure and write the IT partner's share. ROADMAP.md:267 · ADR-026:38

Blocker

Write a BAA template and a one-page HIPAA posture sheet. Every dental buyer asks for them. The integration setup already requires a signed BAA before a real practice system is connected. integrations/SETUP.md §6

High

Verify the export-drop profiles against real exports. Start with Open Dental and Eaglesoft. Dentrix needs a profile written from a real file. export-drop.md

High

Finish Windows signing. Front desks run Windows. Authenticode signing is "wired, not procured", and without it remote access does not work. The installer is NSIS only, with no MSI, which IT partners deploying with Intune or group policy will ask about. droplet-windows/README.md · WARP-2688

High

Close the Eaglesoft legal question (WARP-1100) before promising a live Eaglesoft connection. Until then, sell export-drop only.

Later

Start SOC 2 Type I once 5 practices are live. It opens multi-location groups and small DSOs. The compliance tracker says audits wait for customer demand, and this would be that demand. compliance-progress.md
